# ADDITIONS beyond the scikit-learn list. Kept in a separate, labelled block so
# the vendored provenance above stays exactly what it claims to be, and so a
# reader can see at a glance which terms are ours rather than upstream's.
#
# 's' and 't' — apostrophe fragments.
#
# WORD_RE splits on non-word characters, and an apostrophe is one. So "Trump's"
# becomes ["trump", "s"] and "don't" becomes ["don", "t"]. The fragments are not
# words; they are the debris of a splitting rule.
#
# MEASURED on mind_small (65,238 articles), 2026-08-21:
#   's' appears in 28,339 documents (43.4%)
#   't' appears in  6,300 documents  (9.7%)
#   together they are 51,326 of 1,889,091 English tokens (2.7%)
#
# 's' was the single most frequent term in the entire English corpus, ahead of
# "new" and "said". Two reasons that matters beyond untidiness. It inflates
# document length, which is precisely the quantity BM25's length normalisation
# reacts to, so documents were being penalised in proportion to how many
# possessives their headlines contained. And at 43.4% it sits just under the 50%
# point where Okapi's inverse document frequency turns negative -- close enough
# that the corpus-dependent idf edge case could be triggered by a term carrying
# no meaning at all.
#
# Only 's' and 't' are added, not "every single letter": Danish and English both
# have meaningful one-character tokens in other positions, and D4 explicitly
# rejected a minimum term length because an English-tuned threshold silently
# damages Danish. These two are named because they are artefacts of OUR
# tokenisation rule, not short words that happen to exist.
#
# See design_rationale.md for the alternatives considered.
# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
